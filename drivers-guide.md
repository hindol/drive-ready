# Washington State Driver Guide — Study Summary (for question writing)

Source: Washington State Driver Guide (text-only). This is a paraphrased study outline intended to help generate practice questions; it is not the official guide.

## Chapter 1 — Licenses (high-level)
- Driving is a privilege and a responsibility; build safe habits early.
- New residents with an out-of-state license generally have a limited time window to obtain a WA license; expired out-of-state licenses can trigger retesting.
- Teen licensing includes an instruction-permit phase and an intermediate license phase with passenger/night restrictions that ease over time.
- Driver licensing requires proof of identity; bring original documents.
- Exams: knowledge exam must be passed before the skills exam; knowledge exam validity window affects timing.
- DUI basics are introduced here (BAC/THC thresholds and implied consent concepts appear in the guide).

## Chapter 2 — Vehicles
### Vehicle ownership, registration, plates, insurance
- Vehicle title (certificate of ownership) is an important document and should be kept safe.
- Plates must be displayed correctly and kept readable.
- Drivers must carry proof of required liability insurance; the guide lists minimum coverage amounts.

### Vehicle basics and maintenance
- Adjust seat, steering wheel, and mirrors every time you drive; maintain good visibility and control.
- Tire tread, tire pressure, clean glass, and working lights/turn signals are basic safety checks.
- Headlights: the guide explains when to use headlights and how/when to dim high beams around other traffic.
- Hazard lights and hand signals are covered as backup communication when signals fail.

### Occupant protection
- Seat belts: proper fit and correct wear for all occupants.
- Child restraints: stages (rear-facing, forward-facing/harness, booster, seat belt fit) are based on age/size guidance.
- Airbags: seat belts and airbags work together; children should not ride in the front seat.

## Chapter 3 — Drivers
### Fitness to drive
- Driving requires physical and mental readiness; illness, stress, and impairing substances reduce safe driving ability.

### Impairment and risk
- Alcohol/cannabis/medications can impair; there is no safe amount of cannabis for driving.
- Fatigue and drowsy driving are treated as impairment; recognize warning signs and stop to rest.

### Distraction and attention
- Washington’s distracted-driving rules prohibit holding electronic devices while driving; hands-free is permitted but can still distract.
- Smart driving emphasizes scanning ahead/side/behind, using mirrors, and doing shoulder checks.

### Aggressive driving and road rage
- Aggressive driving is a traffic offense; road rage is treated as criminal behavior.

## Chapter 4 — Roads
### Sharing the road
- Pedestrians: yield at crosswalks and intersections (marked or unmarked). Use extra caution for people using mobility aids or service animals.
- School buses: red lights/extended stop sign require stopping; special rules apply on multi-lane roads with medians/barriers.
- Transit buses: yield when a transit vehicle signals and pulls back into the roadway.
- Large vehicles: avoid blind zones; give extra stopping/turning space; don’t cut in unless you can see both headlights in your mirror.
- Motorcycles: harder to see; don’t share a lane; give extra following distance and check blind zones.
- Bicyclists: bicyclists may use the full lane; when passing, give at least 3 feet and don’t turn across a bicyclist’s path.
- Trains/light rail: trains have right-of-way; obey lights/gates; don’t stop on tracks; stop a safe distance from rails when required.

### Traffic control devices
- Traffic control devices include signals, signs, and road markings; special personnel directions can override signals.
- Broken traffic signals are treated like a 4-way stop.

### Traffic signals
- Meanings of solid/flashing lights and arrows; rules for turning on red depend on sign restrictions and safe gaps.

### Signs
- The guide explains sign color meanings (regulatory/warning/construction/services/recreation/etc.).
- Common regulatory/warning signs (stop/yield/speed limit/one-way/do not enter/wrong way/no passing/roundabout) are summarized.

### Intersections and turning
- Right-of-way principles for 4-way and 2-way stops.
- Turning: signal before turning; turn into the nearest appropriate lane; maintain lane through multi-lane turns.
- Roundabouts: slow, choose lane early, yield to circulating traffic, and watch for pedestrians/bicyclists at crosswalks.

### Road markings and special lanes
- Meanings of white/yellow lines, solid vs dashed, double white barriers, and passing rules.
- Center turn lanes (two-way left-turn lanes) are for left turns/U-turns where permitted; not for passing; limited travel distance.
- HOV, reversible, reserved, and toll lanes have specific markings and overhead signal rules.

### Zones
- School zones: reduced speed (commonly 20 mph) and heightened awareness near schools/playgrounds.
- Work zones: expect lane changes, narrow lanes, and increased risk; fines can be doubled when workers are present.
- Emergency zones: move over or slow down by a specified margin; the guide includes a max-speed condition.

### Parking
- The guide lists “do not park” restrictions (e.g., near signals/signs, crosswalks/sidewalks/bike lanes, railroads, hydrants) and distance rules.
- Parking safety includes setting the parking brake, checking for traffic/bicyclists before opening doors, and special guidance for hills/curbs.

## Chapter 5 — Hazards
- Risk awareness and hazard perception: continuously scan and be ready to change speed/lane/path.
- Speed: speed limits apply to ideal conditions; drivers must adjust speed for conditions.
- Space/time: maintain safe buffers; merging requires creating safe gaps; zipper merging is described for lane closures.
- Conditions: night driving, curves, wet/snow/ice and black ice, skidding and hydroplaning response steps.
- Vehicle failures: tire blowout, engine/power loss, headlight failure, stuck accelerator response guidance.
- Collisions: don’t leave; call emergency services when needed; exchange information; file required reports within the guide’s timeline.
- Law enforcement stops: pull over safely, stay in vehicle unless directed, keep hands visible, follow instructions.

## High-yield numeric rules mentioned in the guide (for question candidates)
- Turn signals: signal about 100 feet before turning.
- High beams: dim for oncoming traffic (about 500 feet) and when following another vehicle (about 300 feet).
- Emergency triangles/flares placement guidance (hundreds of feet behind) is included.
- Bike passing clearance: at least 3 feet.
- Railroad stop distance: stop about 15–50 feet from the nearest rail when required.
- School zone speed: commonly 20 mph (with sign timing conditions).
- DUI thresholds are listed for adults vs. under-21 for alcohol and THC.
- Insurance minimums are listed as dollar amounts.
